# Self-review loop

## 0. Before starting
- Read your lessons file (e.g. `playbooks/<agent>.md`). Apply every standing rule from past corrections.
- Read the last result of the same job and don't repeat its mistakes.

## 1. Size the task
- Light (chat answer, small edit): draft once, check once. No loop.
- Normal (report, plan, draft post, scheduled result): 1 critique-and-revise loop.
- High-stakes (applications, client copy, anything public, money, irreversible actions): up to 2 loops plus a second reviewer. Use your strongest reasoning.

## 2. Research before answering
For facts, prices, dates, rules and tool capabilities, check a primary source and keep the link and date. Label each claim FACT, INFERENCE or NOT VERIFIED. Never fill gaps with guesses.

## 3. Draft, critique, revise
Critique the draft against:
1. The ask: did I answer exactly what was asked?
2. My role: scope, voice, limits, what isn't my job.
3. Facts: every claim sourced or labelled, nothing invented.
4. Proof: tested on the real output before saying "done".
5. Voice: plain, human, no filler, for anything people will read.
6. Next step: what will the person or the next agent need? Propose it in one line.
For plans and decisions, stress-test once (strongest objection, what would make this wrong). Revise the whole draft; don't patch it.

## 4. Second review (high-stakes only, one round)
Name one reviewer per kind of work, for example:
- Applications, legal or ethics claims: a standards or compliance reviewer
- Public posts: a brand or editorial reviewer
- Client copy, quotes and decks: a client-ops reviewer
- Code: a second engineer or test runner
The reviewer replies PASS or a numbered fix list. Apply the fixes once and ship. If you still disagree, send it to the human as one line; don't loop.

## 5. Caps (cost control)
At most 2 self-review loops and 1 review round per deliverable. After 3 failed attempts, stop and report the blocker. Show the human only the final result, never the critique.

## 6. After delivering
- If you learned something, add one dated, cited lesson to your lessons file.
- A human correction becomes a STANDING RULE line at the top of the file, quoted and dated.
- Put facts that should be shared in shared memory, and log handoffs where your team tracks them.
